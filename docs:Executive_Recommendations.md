# Executive Recommendations

Each recommendation below is tied directly to a finding in `Business_Insights.md` and is written the way it would be presented to a leadership team — the action, why it matters, and what it would take to execute.

## 1. Shift Investment Toward Retention Before Scaling Acquisition
**Why:** With NRR at 58%, incremental acquisition spend is currently being offset by losses in the existing base — the business is running to stand still. Closing even half the gap to a 100% NRR benchmark represents roughly $550K+ in monthly recurring revenue recovered without acquiring a single new customer.
**What it takes:** A retention-owner function (or clearer ownership within existing Customer Success/Product teams), a monthly NRR trendline reviewed at the leadership level, and a defined intervention playbook tied to churn reason (competitor, price, budget, product_fit).

## 2. Stress-Test the UK / Health Concentration
**Why:** A meaningful share of revenue sits in one geography and one vertical. This needs to be classified as either a strength to invest further into, or a risk to actively diversify away from — right now it's neither confirmed.
**What it takes:** A segment-level deep-dive comparing UK/Health retention and growth rates against the rest of the portfolio, plus a scenario model of revenue impact if either segment contracts.

## 3. Build an Expansion Motion
**Why:** The narrow gap between NRR (58%) and GRR (53%) shows expansion revenue is barely contributing. This is upside currently left on the table — most SaaS businesses generate several points of NRR lift purely from upsell/cross-sell to the existing base.
**What it takes:** A structured upgrade path with usage-based triggers (from `plan_changes` behavior already tracked), and a sales/success motion incentivized on expansion, not just retention.

## 4. Re-Evaluate Acquisition Channel Mix Once CAC Data Is Available
**Why:** Referral currently leads on revenue, but that's a top-line view — it says nothing about retention quality or cost efficiency by channel. Reallocating budget on revenue alone risks over-investing in a channel that looks good and retains poorly.
**What it takes:** Marketing/sales spend data by channel (currently the single blocking data gap), layered against the existing channel-level retention data to compute a true retention-adjusted CAC.

## 5. Move Churn Reporting to a Monthly Cadence with Cause Attribution
**Why:** Churn causes (competitor, price, budget, product_fit) are evenly distributed — meaning there's no single fix, and progress needs to be tracked by cause, not just by aggregate rate, to know which intervention is working.
**What it takes:** A recurring monthly churn review segmented by reason, tied to the specific team/function that owns each cause (Product for product_fit, Pricing/Finance for price, Competitive Intelligence for competitor).

## Sequencing
Recommendations 1 and 5 are foundational and should start immediately — they require no new data. Recommendation 2 can run in parallel using existing data. Recommendations 3 and 4 are highest-value but sequenced after 1 and 5 establish a retention baseline to measure improvement against.
