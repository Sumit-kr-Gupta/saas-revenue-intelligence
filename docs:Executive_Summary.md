# Executive Summary

## Purpose of This Document
This document expands on the README's headline numbers with the narrative a leadership team or investor would expect: what's working, what's broken, and what it's worth to fix.

## The One-Paragraph Version
The business runs on a $31.4M ARR base generated from 25,000 customers, but only 5,469 of them are currently active revenue-generating accounts. Net Revenue Retention of 58% means the existing customer base is shrinking in dollar terms every month, faster than expansion revenue (upsells/upgrades) can offset it. This is not an acquisition problem — it's a retention problem, and it is the single most valuable lever in the business today.

## Why This Matters More Than It Looks
A company can report growing total revenue while its underlying retention is deteriorating, because new customer acquisition can mask churn in a simple revenue chart. That is close to what's happening here: $3.46M in cumulative revenue and a healthy-looking $2.62M MRR sit on top of an NRR/GRR pair (58%/53%) that would concern any SaaS board. The narrow 5-point gap between NRR and GRR is itself a diagnostic: it says expansion revenue is barely moving the needle, so the fix has to start with stopping the leak, not accelerating upsell.

## What "Good" Would Look Like
Healthy SaaS businesses typically target NRR in the 100–120% range, meaning the existing customer base grows in dollar terms even before any new customers are acquired. At current MRR, closing even half the gap to a 100% NRR benchmark represents roughly $550K+ in monthly recurring revenue — recovered without spending a dollar on new acquisition.

## Where the Business Is Concentrated
Revenue skews toward the UK market and the Health industry vertical, and toward the Premium pricing tier. Concentration isn't inherently bad — it can reflect real product-market fit — but it means the business has meaningful exposure if any one of those segments turns. Revenue Concentration analysis (Top 10 / Top 20 customer revenue share) was built specifically to quantify this exposure at the account level, not just the segment level.
